The key relationship doom loop

Co-founders. Key executives. Manager to direct report. The same argument in a new costume.

Explore different working preferences with Pairs and your shared guides. Name the decision, clarify what each person needs, and prepare how to reopen the conversation.

The team that stays surface-level

You have assembled impressive people, but they are not really syncing. Feedback does not land. People hold back what they actually think.

Begin with the words people choose to share in their guides. Talk explicitly about feedback and disagreement, and use a specific situation to practice a different opening.

Your own development

Self-awareness is a foundation for how you lead. You want to understand your strengths, your edges, and the patterns that return under pressure.

Use your Profile and Deep Read as starting points. Choose one action to try, then bring the result back into reflection with Thea.

The high-stakes conversation you keep avoiding

Your co-founder wants the big picture. Your COO wants the evidence. A recommendation can land differently depending on what the other person needs.

Look at the context you actually have, prepare a clear opening, then rehearse with Thea. Ask a question that leaves room for their response.

Distributed and remote teams

When people work across time zones, cultures, and languages, different expectations can remain invisible.

Use your guides to discuss response times, focus time, decision-making, and how to raise a concern. Make working expectations explicit before distance turns them into assumptions.

Explore your guide

The brilliant mis-hire

Great on paper, difficult in practice. You expected someone to work like you, and they expected to work like themselves. Neither expectation was discussed.

Use an onboarding conversation to understand working preferences and agree how to collaborate. SuperQ’s readings are for development and collaboration, not hiring or performance decisions.

The gap nobody talks about

Inclusion means people feel seen as individuals, as well as represented. One way of leading does not make room for every way of contributing.

Ask how each person prefers to prepare and contribute. Make space for quieter voices, reflection, and different communication styles.

Explore your guide

The manager who leads everyone the same way

Good managers adapt to how each person needs to be led. That starts with asking, rather than assuming everyone needs what you need.

Use authored guide lines and shared context to discuss feedback, decisions, and support. Prepare for a real conversation and check what helps against experience.
